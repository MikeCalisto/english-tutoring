PAST SIMPLE

Last Monday, Lisa and her husband fed the children when he came home.

PAST CONTINUOUS

Review of Future Forms

which form for which meaning
🔮
future simple

a) in predictions about the future, usually with think, believe, be afraid, probably. I believe life will change in the 21st century.

b) for on-the-spot decisions or offers. It's late. I'll take a taxi home. I'll lend you my car if you want.

c) for actions, events or situations which will definitely happen in the future and which we cannot control. Winter will set in early this year.

d) for promises, threats, warnings, requests, hopes. Stop talking or I'll send you out of the class.

🎯
be going to

a) for plans, intentions or ambitions. I'm going to buy a sports car.

b) in predictions when there is evidence that something will happen in the near future. Someone's going to fall over that box if you don't move it.

📅
present continuous

for fixed arrangements in the near future. He's flying to Turkey next month. He has already booked his flight.

🚂
present simple

for timetables and programmes. The ferry leaves at eight o'clock tomorrow morning.

⏱️
future continuous

a) for actions which will be in progress at a stated future time. This time next week he'll be flying to Morocco.

b) for actions which will definitely happen as a result of a routine or arrangement. I'll be seeing Jim at work later on today.

c) when we ask politely about someone's plans for the near future. Will you be going to the dentist this afternoon?

✅
future perfect

for actions which will be finished before a stated future time. We will have returned home by Sunday afternoon.

⏳
future perfect continuous

to emphasise the duration of an action up to a certain time in the future. By the end of this week, she will have been working here for six years.

ENGLISH TUTORING PLATFORM

Future forms — note

four rules that decide the form
🎉
We use the Present Continuous rather than be going to for things which are definitely arranged to happen in the future

They're having a party next week.

They're going to have a party in two weeks.

It's all arranged, the invitations have already been sent. — They've decided but it hasn't been arranged yet.
🕒
We use the Present Simple for timetables, programmes, etc.

Our plane leaves at 10:30 am.

⛔
We do not use the Future tense after while, before, until, as soon as, if (conditional) and when (time conjunction)

Call me when you arrive.

BUT When will you be ready?

We can use when + will if when is a question word.
🚶
With the verbs go and come we often use the Present Continuous rather than be going to

I'm going out tonight.

RATHER THAN I'm going to go out tonight.

Future Perfect and time clauses

what tense goes after by the time, until, before

After the time expressions by the time, until, before we use the present simple, because they introduce time clauses. The future perfect and the future perfect continuous may come either before or after the time clause.

TIME CLAUSE SECOND

I will have tidied up by the time you get back.

The main clause is in the future perfect, the time clause after 'by the time' is in the present simple.
TIME CLAUSE FIRST

By the time she finishes work, we will have been waiting for more than an hour.

The time clause comes first and is still in the present simple; the main clause is in the future perfect continuous.

Shakespeare is now dead; he won't write again.
past habit or state

He used to go / went to school on foot.

Past Continuous
two or more simultaneous past actions

While I was sunbathing, Tim was swimming.

action in progress at a stated time in the past

He was playing tennis at 4:30 pm yesterday.

past action in progress interrupted by another past action

While I was getting dressed the bell rang.

The longer action is in the Past Continuous, the shorter action is in the Past Simple.
background description to events in a story

She was flying to Paris. The sun was shining...

Past Perfect
past action which occurred before another past action or before a specific time in the past

He had left by the time I got there.

or by 8:15 pm
complete past action which had visible results in the past

She was sad because she had failed the test.

the Past Perfect is the past equivalent of the Present Perfect

He can't find his watch. He has lost it.

He couldn't find his watch. He had lost it.

Past Perfect Continuous
emphasis on the duration of an action which started and finished in the past before another past action or a stated time in the past

She had been working as a clerk for 10 years before she resigned.

past action of certain duration which had visible results in the past

They were wet because they had been walking in the rain.

the Past Perfect Continuous is the past equivalent of the Present Perfect Continuous

She is going to the doctor. Her leg has been aching for two days.

She went to the doctor. Her leg had been aching for two days.

Future Perfect
